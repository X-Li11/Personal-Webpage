# Why toy calibration for stat-only uses observed data

## The initial intuition

$q_{95}(\Gamma_H^{\rm truth})$ ought to be a property of the hypothesis
$H(\Gamma_H^{\rm truth})$ alone, independent of what data we happened to observe.

## Where that intuition is correct

For the **full profile likelihood** (paper Eq. 7),

$$
t(\Gamma_H) \;=\; -2\ln\frac{L(\Gamma_H,\ \hat{\hat\mu},\ \hat{\hat\theta})}{L(\hat\Gamma_H,\ \hat\mu,\ \hat\theta)},
$$

Wilks says $t\to\chi^2(1)$ asymptotically, **independent of where the toy generation
sets the NPs**. NPs are profiled in both numerator and denominator, so any offset in
their true value asymptotically cancels. So for the All mode, calibration can use
$\theta = 0$, $\theta = $ MLE, or anything else — the resulting $q$ distribution is
the same.

Our All-mode $q_{95}$ matches the colleague's to sub-percent for this reason.

## Where the intuition breaks — modified test statistics

For **stat-only** (and FixTh), the test statistic *freezes* some NPs at a chosen
value:

$$
t_{\rm stat}(\Gamma_H) \;=\; -2\ln\frac{L(\Gamma_H,\ \hat{\hat\mu},\ \theta^{\rm frozen})}{L(\hat\Gamma_H,\ \hat\mu,\ \theta^{\rm frozen})}.
$$

This is **not** the profile likelihood. Its distribution under $H(\Gamma_H^{\rm truth})$
depends on the specific value used for $\theta^{\rm frozen}$. Different choices
produce different distributions:

| Choice of $\theta^{\rm frozen}$ | q distribution | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| NPs = 0 (nominal) | tight, Wilks-like | only truly right if the priors were $\delta$ at 0 |
| Each toy's own uncond MLE (varies per toy) | **fat-tailed** | per-toy scatter in frozen value adds spread |
| Data-derived conditional MLE $\hat{\hat\theta}(\Gamma_H^{\rm truth})$ (same across toys) | Wilks-like | standard "profile construction" (CCGV 2011) |

The last row is the colleague's choice.

## Why the profile-construction choice works

**Cowan-Cranmer-Gross-Vitells (2011, Eur. Phys. J. C 71:1554)** — asymptotic formulae for
likelihood-based tests:

- Under $H(\Gamma_H^{\rm truth})$, the conditional MLE $\hat{\hat\theta}(\Gamma_H^{\rm truth})$
  is a plug-in estimator of true $\theta$.
- Asymptotically $\hat{\hat\theta}\to\theta_{\rm true}$ at rate $O(1/\sqrt{N})$.
- So toys generated at $\hat{\hat\theta}$ are asymptotically indistinguishable from
  toys generated at the truth.
- Under these toys, $t_{\rm stat}$ recovers its asymptotic $\chi^2(1)$ distribution.
- The resulting Neyman construction has correct frequentist coverage, up to
  $O(1/\sqrt{N})$ corrections.

**The observed-data dependence is not a bug**: it's how we correctly calibrate a
modified test statistic when we don't know the true NP values.

## Why it doesn't break frequentist independence

- Data enters only through the choice of a nuisance-parameter reference value, not
  through the parameter of interest.
- $\hat{\hat\theta}$ is itself a random variable over hypothetical repetitions;
  asymptotically it converges to truth with $\sigma\sim 1/\sqrt{N}$.
- Coverage of the resulting CI is preserved to $O(1/\sqrt{N})$, matching the
  Wilks/Chernoff accuracy for the All mode.

If real data changes (recalibration, new dataset), $\hat{\hat\theta}$ shifts slightly
and $q_{95}$ shifts slightly with it. In practice this shift is much smaller than
the toy statistical uncertainty on the quantile.

## Colleague's stat-only procedure — full recipe

For each $\Gamma_H^{\rm truth}$:

**Setup step (uses real data — once per target)**

1. Fit **real data** conditional on $\Gamma_H = \Gamma_H^{\rm truth}$, all NPs floating
   $\Longrightarrow$ conditional MLE $\hat{\hat\theta}(\Gamma_H^{\rm truth})$.
2. Load a snapshot with $\theta = \hat{\hat\theta}(\Gamma_H^{\rm truth})$ AND
   $\alpha_{\rm gobs} = \hat{\hat\theta}(\Gamma_H^{\rm truth})$ (constraint centres
   match the NP values).

**Toy loop (many toys)**

3. Generate a toy from the PDF at this state. Global observables are
   **not resampled** — they stay at the snapshot values.
4. Toy fit:
   - Conditional fit at $\Gamma_H^{\rm truth}$ with all NPs floating
     $\Longrightarrow$ per-toy $\hat{\hat\theta}^{\rm toy}$ (close to the snapshot value).
   - Fix systematic NPs at $\hat{\hat\theta}^{\rm toy}$; second conditional fit at
     $\Gamma_H^{\rm truth}$ with only stat-only NPs floating
     $\Longrightarrow$ $\mathrm{NLL}_{\rm cond}^{\rm toy}$.
5. $q^{\rm toy}(\Gamma_H^{\rm truth})$ from the second fit.

**Extract**: 68th / 95th percentile of $\{q^{\rm toy}\}$ = $q_{68}$, $q_{95}$.

Because per-toy $\hat{\hat\theta}^{\rm toy}$ scatters only weakly around the shared
snapshot value, the frozen $\theta$ is nearly the same across toys, and
$q^{\rm toy}$ follows $\chi^2(1)$ (up to Chernoff near boundary).

## Our (broken) procedure — for contrast

For each $\Gamma_H^{\rm truth}$:

1. `quickToy -s raw`: NPs = 0, global observables **freshly sampled**
   $\alpha_{\rm gobs}\sim N(0,1)$ per toy.
2. Toy fit: uncond fit $\Longrightarrow$ per-toy $\hat\theta^{\rm toy}$ scatters
   widely around 0 (following the sampled $\alpha_{\rm gobs}$).
3. Freeze all systematic NPs at $\hat\theta^{\rm toy}$; cond fit at
   $\Gamma_H^{\rm truth}$ $\Longrightarrow$ $q^{\rm toy}$.

The frozen value **varies wildly toy-to-toy** (via sampled $\alpha_{\rm gobs}$
$\to$ scattered $\hat\theta^{\rm toy}$). That extra spread propagates into $q^{\rm toy}$
as a fat tail; $q_{95}$ inflates well beyond $\chi^2(1)$ expectation.

Empirically: $q_{95}^{\rm Stat, ours}$ reaches 22 at $\Gamma_H = 350$ MeV, while
colleague's stays near 3.5. Observed $q_{\rm obs}$ never crosses ours — no 95% CI
in our grid. Same physics, wrong procedure.

## Summary

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Should the "hypothesis-level" q distribution depend on data? | Ideally no — but for a modified test statistic there is no ideal choice, and profile construction is the standard way to eliminate the extra dependence introduced by the modification. |
| Does the All-mode q depend on data? | No, asymptotically (Wilks). Any NP snapshot is fine. |
| Does the stat-only q depend on data? | Yes, through $\hat{\hat\theta}(\Gamma_H^{\rm truth})$, by design. Ensures Wilks-consistent coverage. |
| Is coverage preserved? | Yes, to $O(1/\sqrt{N})$ (proven asymptotically by CCGV 2011). |
| Why did our procedure fail? | Our freeze value scattered per toy (from GObs sampling + per-toy MLE). Colleague's freeze value is shared across toys → Wilks-like q. |



# Toy calibration procedures for the H→γγ width test statistic

## Common definitions

Profile-likelihood test statistic (paper Eq. 7):

$$
t(\Gamma_H) \;=\; -2\ln\frac{L(\Gamma_H,\ \hat{\hat\mu},\ \hat{\hat\theta})}{L(\hat\Gamma_H,\ \hat\mu,\ \hat\theta)}
$$

Single-hats = unconditional MLE. Double-hats = conditional MLE with $\Gamma_H$ fixed.

For each $\Gamma_H^{\rm truth}$ point on a grid, we generate toys and compute $q^{\rm toy}$
to build a calibration curve $q_{95}(\Gamma_H^{\rm truth})$, $q_{68}(\Gamma_H^{\rm truth})$.

Modes we consider:
- **All (Total)**: uncond fits $\Gamma_H,\mu,\theta$ all floating; cond fits $\Gamma_H$ fixed, $\mu,\theta$ float.
- **FixTh**: same fits, but during the cond fit theory NPs $\theta_{\rm theory}$ are frozen at a fixed value (see below for *which* value).
- **Stat only**: same, all ATLAS NPs frozen at a fixed value.

Where we differ from colleague is which "fixed value" the toys are anchored to.

---

## Colleague's procedure (produces Wilks-consistent q, fast)

For each $\Gamma_H^{\rm truth}$:

### Toy generation

1. Fit real data conditional on $\Gamma_H = \Gamma_H^{\rm truth}$, all NPs floating $\Longrightarrow$ **data-derived MLE $\hat{\hat\theta}(\Gamma_H^{\rm truth})$**.
2. Snapshot: load NPs and global observables both at $\hat{\hat\theta}(\Gamma_H^{\rm truth})$. Global obs are NOT resampled per toy.
3. Generate $N$ toys from PDF evaluated at this snapshot.

### Toy fit (per mode)

- **Total**: standard uncond + cond profile fits. $q^{\rm toy}$ under $H(\Gamma_H^{\rm truth})$ is $\chi^2(1)$-like by Wilks (Chernoff near boundary).
- **Stat only**:
  1. Cond fit at $\Gamma_H^{\rm truth}$ with all NPs floating $\Longrightarrow$ per-toy $\hat{\hat\theta}^{\rm toy}$.
  2. Fix systematic NPs at $\hat{\hat\theta}^{\rm toy}$; do a second cond fit at $\Gamma_H^{\rm truth}$ with only stat NPs floating $\Longrightarrow$ NLL$_{\rm cond}^{\rm toy}$.
  3. $q^{\rm toy}$ from this second fit.

Because the toys were *generated* at the data-consistent state, per-toy $\hat{\hat\theta}^{\rm toy}$ are all close to $\hat{\hat\theta}(\Gamma_H^{\rm truth})$. Toy-to-toy variability in the frozen value is small. $q^{\rm toy}$ distribution follows $\chi^2(1)$.

### Coverage result

Actual $P(q > 3.84) \approx 0.05$, $P(q > 1) \approx 0.317$ for $\Gamma_H \gtrsim 30$ MeV. Chernoff correction reduces both near the boundary. Same behavior in Total and Stat modes.

### Cost

One toy campaign per $\Gamma_H^{\rm truth}$. Fits per toy: 2–3. No per-toy MLE-snapshot bookkeeping needed (systematic NP freeze value comes from the toy's own uncond fit, which is a natural quickFit output).

## Our (broken) procedure

For each $\Gamma_H^{\rm truth}$:

### Toy generation

1. Snapshot `raw`: NPs and global obs all at 0 (nominal).
2. `quickToy` generates $N$ toys with:
   - NPs held at 0 during PDF evaluation.
   - Global observables *freshly sampled* $\alpha_{\rm gobs} \sim N(0, 1)$ per toy.

### Toy fit (Stat mode)

1. Uncond fit with all NPs floating. Because per-toy $\alpha_{\rm gobs}$ scattered widely, per-toy $\hat\theta^{\rm toy}$ scatters widely around 0 too.
2. Freeze all ATLAS NPs at $\hat\theta^{\rm toy}$ (per-toy, different every time).
3. Cond fit at $\Gamma_H^{\rm truth}$ with frozen NPs. Compute $q^{\rm toy}$.

### Why the tail is fat

Two independent sources of scatter get injected:

- **Toy-generation scatter**: $\alpha_{\rm gobs}$ sampled fresh per toy $\Longrightarrow$ $\hat\theta^{\rm toy}$ varies $\sim 1\sigma$.
- **Freeze-at-per-toy-MLE**: the "frozen" value in the cond fit varies per toy by the same $\sim 1\sigma$.

Under $H(\Gamma_H^{\rm truth})$, this makes the frozen $\theta$ often "wrong" relative to what the toy's data at that hypothesis would prefer, inflating NLL$_{\rm cond}$ toy-to-toy. Result: $q^{\rm toy}$ distribution has a much heavier tail than $\chi^2(1)$; $P(q > 3.84) \sim 0.15$–0.20; $q_{95}$ inflates to 10–20 for our Stat mode.

### Coverage result

$q_{95}^{\rm Stat}$ scales linearly with $\Gamma_H^{\rm truth}$ (up to ~22 at $\Gamma_H = 350$). Observed $q_{\rm obs}$ never crosses $\Longrightarrow$ CI reported as "$>350$ MeV" — nonsense compared to colleague's Wilks-consistent behavior.

---

## Fix — reproduce colleague's procedure

Two changes to our pipeline:

**(a) Toy generation**: instead of `raw`, load a *per-$\Gamma_H$* snapshot with NPs and global observables at $\hat{\hat\theta}(\Gamma_H^{\rm truth})$.

Implementation: a preliminary loop that, for each $\Gamma_H^{\rm truth}$, fits real data conditional on that value with all NPs floating; saves the post-fit workspace with a snapshot; then `quickToy` uses that snapshot as its starting state. Global observables should be pinned to the data-conditional values (NOT resampled per toy).

**(b) Toy fit for Stat mode**: replace our "uncond + freeze at per-toy MLE + cond" 5-fit pipeline with the colleague's simpler 2-fit chain:

1. Cond fit with all NPs floating $\Longrightarrow$ per-toy $\hat{\hat\theta}^{\rm toy}$ near the snapshot.
2. Cond fit with systematic NPs frozen at $\hat{\hat\theta}^{\rm toy}$, only stat NPs floating $\Longrightarrow$ NLL$_{\rm cond}^{\rm toy}$.

The $q^{\rm toy}$ from step 2 will then be Wilks-consistent because the frozen values don't scatter wildly.
